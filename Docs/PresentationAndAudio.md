# Presentation and narration exports

Assembly on PC and mobile places parts immediately. Difficulty, recipe order, socket validation and manual line of sight still apply. Next Step installs one eligible part immediately. Engine Display alone animates short final insertion strokes, with components already oriented and appearing near their fitting positions. Scrubbing backwards reverses those strokes.

Menus, chapter cards, pause/settings, instructions and mobile panels share the light `WorkshopTheme` palette and custom rounded cards. PC chapter photographs use a rounded mask and centered cover crop which adapts to the image and card aspect ratios. `CroppedArtwork` preserves image proportions.

The separate `EngineSurfaceLibrary` resource applies material choices to labelled engine renderers when a scene loads. Existing meshes, collider prefabs and saved scenes retain their source materials. The triplanar URP shader works on CAD meshes without UV coordinates, with cast/machined metal, bearing alloy, motor housing and rubber surfaces. Regenerate the separate palette and default chapter previews with **Tools → Engine Assembly → Presentation → Create Light Artwork and Surface Palette** outside Play mode. A manually assigned photograph in the original chapter artwork catalogue takes precedence over its supplied alternative.

Texture sources are ambientCG [Metal006](https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Metal006), [Metal012](https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Metal012) and [Rubber004](https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Rubber004), distributed under CC0. Their color, roughness and OpenGL normal maps are in `Assets/Art/Textures/ambientCG`; `sources.json` records the download sources. `Tools/Art/download_materials.py` retrieves those maps.

English narration exports are in **Assets/Audio/Narration/English**, divided into **Assembly** and **Repair**. `manifest.json` maps cue IDs to relative WAV paths, full instruction text, a generation-settings/text hash, sample rate and duration. The files are 24 kHz mono PCM WAV. `Assets/Resources/NarrationLibrary.asset` connects the 135 unique clips to runtime playback: PC, phone and repair pause with their menus and replace the instruction when its cue changes. Matching piston and valve parts share a generic instruction that plays once per chapter visit. After the first of a repeated type or unit is done, a short reminder says to repeat it; later matches are silent. Different component types have separate cues. PC flips and the first matching-item inventory stack have their own voice lines. Press R on PC to replay the most recent instruction. Captions remain visible. Narration uses a 2D source so walking distance and AR camera positions cannot make it inaudible. The volume sliders in PC settings and main-menu options share a saved master volume. After regenerating narration, run **Tools → Engine Assembly → Audio → Connect Exported Narration** to update the library.

The assembled engine, loose pieces, subassemblies and socket frames use 60% of the previous linear scale; reloading a recipe does not scale them again. PC assembly enforces the recipe's upright/upside-down destination requirements; phone/AR accepts eligible placement in any engine orientation. The PC crosshair inverts the rendered color beneath its strokes. Its inventory count has a separate white outline material, leaving the shared fonts unchanged.

Click or E picks up one PC part at a time. Matching parts can still be collected individually into the same inventory stack. Valve hints recommend all eligible pieces of each family (seats, seals, springs, retainers, keepers) before the next family, without adding new placement restrictions to equal-priority parts.

The generator uses the virtual environment in `C:/Users/Vedant/AI_Models/qwen3tts` and leaves that project's files unchanged. It takes `test_voice.wav` from the original `main.py` as the single primary reference, with its known transcript. The official Qwen 1.7B Base clone checkpoint runs locally on CUDA. It extracts acoustic codes and a speaker embedding once, saves them in `Tools/Narration/Voice/voice_clone_prompt.pt`, and explicitly passes that same prompt into every generation. It uses temperature 0.7, repetition penalty 1.1 and seed 1234 plus the cue index. Resuming a run reloads the saved prompt and verifies completed audio hashes. This implements [Qwen's reusable clone-prompt workflow](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS#voice-design-then-clone); the illustrative `get_speaker_embedding`/`speaker_embedding` calls are not APIs of this installed package.

Unchanged exports keep their existing filenames and Unity metadata/GUIDs. Shared cues use their own canonical filenames. The previous locked-voice set is backed up in `Backups/Narration/20261013-194138-LockedVoiceBeforeSharedCues`. The 183 assembly instruction steps map to 135 unique voices, including repair, completion, flip and reminder cues. Run from the Unity project:

`Tools/Narration/progress.json` reports generation status. `Tools/Narration/verify_audio.py` checks every expected cue and writes `audio-verification.json`. The source instructions are `Assets/Resources/EngineLearning.json` and `Docs/RepairNarration.txt`, with chapter/repair completion cues added by the generator. Obsolete compulsory orientation instructions are removed to match the captions and orientation-independent mobile placement.
