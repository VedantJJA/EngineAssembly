ENGINE LAB - BEGINNER ASSEMBLY LESSONS

Each lesson explains the purpose first, then identifies the part and its fitting position. Part labels distinguish similar sizes; location descriptions stay relative to the engine when it is turned. Read or replay any lesson whenever needed.

BLOCK AND CRANKSHAFT

chapter-1-cue-001 - unique lesson
An engine turns the pressure of burning fuel into rotation. We will build the moving parts first, then the valves, belts and fuel system. The cylinder block is the engine's main body. Pick up the largest hollow casting, the cylinder block. Its three large round holes guide the pistons. Place it on the main engine lift with these holes facing up. The open space underneath is where the crankshaft will fit.

chapter-1-cue-002 - unique lesson
A bearing lets a shaft turn with less rubbing. This smaller needle bearing contains many thin rollers inside a metal ring. Find the smaller of the two complete crankshaft bearing rings. Fit it into the smaller round support at one end of the block's open underside. Keep the ring's hole along the length of the block, where the crankshaft will run.

chapter-1-cue-168 - unique lesson
The larger needle bearing supports the other end of the crankshaft. Its rollers carry the load while allowing rotation. Find the larger complete bearing ring. Fit it into the larger round end support in the block's open underside. Its centre hole must line up with the smaller bearing at the other end.

chapter-1-cue-169 - unique lesson
The bearing shells are half rings. They form smooth surfaces between the crankshaft and the block. Look between the two end bearings. Place one curved shell in each of the four semicircular supports across the open underside. The hollow side of each shell faces the crankshaft. The other halves are fitted later.

chapter-1-cue-003 - unique lesson
The crankshaft changes the pistons' up-and-down movement into rotation. The smooth round sections are its bearing surfaces; the offset sections will connect to the rods. Pick up the long, unevenly shaped crankshaft. Lay it lengthwise in the open underside of the block. Centre its smooth bearing surfaces in the shells and rings you just fitted. Both ends project from the block.

PISTONS AND CONNECTING RODS

chapter-2-cue-004 - piston-201
A piston is a short metal cylinder. Burning gas pushes on its flat top, and the connecting rod passes this force to the crankshaft. We build this small unit separately before putting it in the engine. Find a piston on the nearby parts table. Put it on its separate work area with the flat top down and the hollow underside open toward you. You should be able to see the two holes through its sides.

chapter-2-cue-005 - piston-202
The connecting rod links the piston to the crankshaft. Its small end pivots inside the piston; its large, split end will wrap around the crankshaft. Pick up the rod with a small hole at one end and a wide half-circle at the other. Put the small end inside the hollow piston. Line up its hole with the two holes through the piston sides. Leave the wide end outside.

chapter-2-cue-006 - piston-203
The wrist pin is the hinge between the piston and its rod. It lets the rod change angle as the crankshaft turns. Find the short, smooth cylindrical pin. Slide it sideways through one piston hole, through the small hole in the connecting rod, and into the opposite piston hole. Centre the pin inside the piston before fitting its clips.

chapter-2-cue-007 - piston-204
The two wrist-pin clips stop the hinge pin sliding out and scraping the cylinder wall. A circlip is a small split ring that sits in a groove. Fit one small split ring into the groove just inside each end of the piston pin hole. There are two clips, one on each side of the piston. Both sit outside the ends of the installed pin.

chapter-2-cue-008 - piston-206
A stud is a threaded rod without a bolt head. These two studs will hold the connecting rod's separate cap when the piston is inside the engine. At the wide, open end of the connecting rod, find the two holes beside the half-circle. Fit one stud into each hole. Leave the separate curved cap off for now so the rod can pass around the crankshaft later.

chapter-2-cue-009 - piston-207
The oil-control ring spreads a thin lubricating film and removes excess oil from the cylinder wall. It is different from the two rings that seal the gas pressure. Find the oil-control ring. Place it around the piston in the groove nearest the hollow underside and connecting rod. Describe the groove by the piston itself, not by its height above the floor, because the piston may be upside down.

chapter-2-cue-010 - piston-208
The second ring helps scrape excess oil down the cylinder wall and supports the sealing work of the ring above it. Despite its name, oil control is an important job of this compression ring. Find the ring labelled second compression ring. Fit it around the piston in the middle of the three outside grooves. The oil-control ring is on one side of this groove; the flat piston top is on the other.

chapter-2-cue-011 - piston-209
The top compression ring provides the first seal against the hot gas above the piston. With all three rings fitted, this piston unit is ready for the engine. Find the top compression ring and fit it into the outside groove closest to the flat piston top. Check the order from that flat top: top compression ring, second compression ring, then oil-control ring.

chapter-2-cue-028 - unique lesson
The three piston units work inside the block. Each connecting rod transfers the piston force to one offset section of the crankshaft. Pick up a completed piston-and-rod unit. Insert the rod end first into one of the three large cylinder holes. The flat piston top stays toward the cylinder-head side of the block, and the rod reaches the crankshaft underneath. Fit the other units into the remaining cylinders.

chapter-2-cue-029 - unique lesson
A connecting-rod cap completes the large round end of a rod. It surrounds a crankshaft bearing surface while still allowing the joint to turn. Look into the underside of the block. Find the open half-circle at the end of each connecting rod. Fit its curved cap against it, around the crankshaft, with the two cap holes over the rod's studs. Keep each cap with its own rod.

chapter-2-cue-030 - unique lesson
The nuts hold each connecting-rod cap onto its studs. The cap must stay attached while the engine turns. At each rod cap, find the two threaded stud ends projecting through the cap. Fit one nut on each end. Complete both nuts on all three rods. In this simulation, placing a fastener represents fitting it; it does not teach a real tightening torque.

LOWER CRANKCASE AND OIL SUMP

chapter-3-cue-031 - unique lesson
The lower bearing shells complete the supports around the crankshaft. Their smooth curved faces reduce rubbing against the rotating shaft. Fit the four curved lower shells against the exposed halves of the crankshaft's main bearing surfaces. Each pairs with an upper shell already in the block. Leave their smooth, hollow faces toward the shaft.

chapter-3-cue-032 - unique lesson
The bedplate is the rigid lower frame that holds the crankshaft supports together. Find the large open frame, rather than the bowl-shaped sump. Place it across the underside of the block, over the lower bearing shells. Its long edges line up with the block edges and its bolt holes line up with the supports.

chapter-3-cue-033 - unique lesson
The long bedplate bolts clamp the lower support frame to the block so the crankshaft stays supported. Choose the bedplate bolts labelled M10 by 100. M10 identifies the thread size; 100 is the length in millimetres. Fit them through the deeper bolt holes of the bedplate into the block. Keep the shorter M8 bolts for the next group.

chapter-3-cue-170 - unique lesson
The smaller bedplate bolts secure the remaining attachment points around the lower frame. Choose the bolts labelled M8 by 80. Insert them through the remaining bedplate bolt holes into the block. Their heads rest on the outside face of the frame. Do not use the M8 head-cover bolts from a later chapter.

chapter-3-cue-034 - unique lesson
The oil sump is the bowl that collects lubricating oil beneath the engine. Oil helps moving parts slide without damaging one another. Find the large bowl-shaped sump on the parts table. Place it in its separate work area with the open side accessible. We will add the plate inside the bowl before attaching the whole sump to the engine.

chapter-3-cue-035 - unique lesson
The baffle is a plate that reduces oil sloshing inside the sump as the engine or vehicle moves. Lower the flat baffle plate into the open sump. Its four small screw holes line up with the four raised attachment points inside the bowl. Keep the broad plate inside the bowl, below its outer rim.

chapter-3-cue-036 - unique lesson
The four short screws hold the baffle in the sump. Choose the small M4 by 5 screws for the baffle. Put one through each of the four holes in the plate into the support beneath it. After all four are fitted, the sump and baffle can be carried together.

chapter-3-cue-037 - unique lesson
The completed sump closes the bottom of the engine and provides its oil reservoir. Pick up the whole sump-and-baffle unit. Bring its open rim against the underside of the bedplate. The bowl projects away from the engine and the baffle stays inside. Line up the perimeter holes before fitting the fasteners.

chapter-3-cue-038 - unique lesson
The large sump fastener fits a much wider threaded opening than the smaller attachment bolts. Its diameter helps you distinguish its position from the holes around the rim. Choose the M16 by 55 bolt. Find the large opening on the bottom region of the sump, toward the flywheel end, rather than a small rim hole. Place its threaded end into that opening and leave its head outside.

chapter-3-cue-171 - unique lesson
The short bolt secures one of the shallow attachment points on the sump rim. Choose the M8 by 30 bolt. Find the sump rim corner on the intake side nearest the flywheel. Insert the threaded end through this shallow support toward the engine, leaving the head on the outer face.

chapter-3-cue-172 - unique lesson
The set of bolts clamps the sump rim against the engine at equal-depth attachment points. Choose the four M8 by 35 bolts. Their four holes run across the timing end of the sump rim, the end opposite the flywheel. Place one bolt in each hole with its head outside the rim.

chapter-3-cue-173 - unique lesson
The sump has supports of different depths, so its mounting bolts are not all the same length. Choose the M8 by 40 bolt. Find the sump rim corner on the exhaust side nearest the flywheel. Fit the bolt through this support toward the engine and seat its head against the outside face.

chapter-3-cue-174 - unique lesson
The bolt reaches through a deeper sump support than the shorter bolts you have just placed. Choose the M8 by 45 bolt. On the exhaust-side edge of the sump, move inward from the flywheel corner to the next mounting support. Fit the bolt through that support, with its head outside.

chapter-3-cue-175 - unique lesson
The longest sump bolts pass through the deepest attachment supports to reach the engine above. Choose the three long M8 by 80 sump bolts. Two go through deep supports on opposite long sides, toward the timing half of the sump. The third goes through the deeper intake-side support nearer the flywheel. Keep all heads on the outside of the supports.

chapter-3-cue-039 - unique lesson
The flywheel is a heavy disc that stores rotational energy. It helps the crankshaft turn smoothly between the pushes from the pistons. Find the large round disc. Fit its centre onto the exposed crankshaft end opposite the timing pulleys. Its flat mounting face sits against the crankshaft flange, and the ring of small bolt holes surrounds the shaft centre.

chapter-3-cue-040 - unique lesson
The retaining plate spreads the force of the flywheel bolts around the centre of the disc. Find the smaller flat round plate with several bolt holes. Place it over the centre of the installed flywheel. Line up its holes with the holes in the flywheel and crankshaft flange underneath.

chapter-3-cue-041 - unique lesson
The six bolts lock the flywheel and retaining plate to the crankshaft. Choose the six M10 by 20 flywheel bolts. Place one through each hole in the small retaining plate at the centre of the flywheel. The bolt heads face away from the engine.

CYLINDER HEAD AND VALVES

chapter-4-cue-042 - unique lesson
The cylinder head closes the tops of the cylinders. Its valves let fresh air in and exhaust gas out. Each of the three chambers has four valve openings. Find the long rectangular cylinder head and place it on its separate work area. Look at the face with three shallow chamber shapes. The small holes inside each chamber are where the valve stems pass through. We will build the valves here before moving the head onto the engine.

chapter-4-cue-043 - unique lesson
An exhaust valve opens to let burnt gas leave a cylinder. It looks like a long thin stem with a flat round head at one end. Choose the exhaust valves, using their part labels to distinguish them from intake valves. From the chamber face of the loose cylinder head, slide each stem into an exhaust guide hole. The round valve head stays in the chamber and closes against its circular seat. Fit the exhaust pair in each chamber.

chapter-4-cue-176 - unique lesson
An intake valve opens to let fresh air enter a cylinder. When it closes, it helps seal the gas above the piston. Choose the intake valves. From the chamber face of the head, slide their stems through the remaining intake guide holes. Keep the wide round heads on the chamber side. Each chamber now has two intake and two exhaust valves.

chapter-4-cue-044 - valve-402
The spring seat is a thin metal ring that gives the valve spring a firm surface to push against. It protects the cylinder head from the spring end. On the opposite side from the flat valve faces, find a valve stem sticking out of its short guide tube. Slide the thin spring-seat ring down around that tube until it rests flat on the cylinder head.

chapter-4-cue-045 - valve-403
The stem seal controls how much oil can run down the valve stem. Too much oil entering the air passage would be burned in the cylinder. Find the tiny cap-shaped seal with a hole through it. Slide it over the exposed valve stem and down onto the top of the guide tube, inside the spring-seat ring. Keep the hole centred on the stem.

chapter-4-cue-046 - valve-405
The valve spring pushes the valve closed after the camshaft releases it. It must surround the same stem as the seat and seal. Pick up the coil spring. Slide it over the valve stem and seal. Rest its lower end on the flat spring-seat ring already on the head. The stem remains visible through the centre of the spring.

chapter-4-cue-047 - valve-406
The retainer is the small cap that holds the top of the valve spring. It will be locked to the stem by two tiny keeper halves. Find the cap-shaped spring retainer. Place it over the top end of the spring, with the valve stem through its centre hole. The wider face covers the spring end; leave the groove near the stem tip accessible for the keepers.

chapter-4-cue-048 - valve-407
The two keeper halves lock into the stem groove and hold the spring retainer in place. Together they form a small collar around the stem. Find the two tiny half-collars. Put one on each side of the groove near the valve-stem tip, inside the retainer's centre opening. Both halves belong around the same stem. This completes that valve's spring stack.
