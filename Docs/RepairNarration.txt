TIMING IDLER BEARING REPLACEMENT

Replace one faulty bearing while keeping the reusable parts. A circlip is a small split retaining ring that fits in a groove. The shaft rings keep the pulley on its support; the bore rings keep the bearings inside the pulley. Follow the labelled parts in this order.

Step 1. Find the toothed timing belt around the pulleys at the timing end. Pick up the belt and press G to set it on the parts table.

Step 2. Find timing idler 1, the small guide pulley. Remove the split retaining ring at its exposed shaft end and press G. Leave the ring behind the pulley fitted.

Step 3. Pull timing idler 1 off its shaft as a complete unit. Press G to put it on the small work platform.

Step 4. At the small platform, find the split ring inside the pulley opening. Remove the first labelled ring and press G. These two rings hold the bearings inside the pulley.

Step 5. Remove the second labelled ring from the pulley opening and press G. Both bearing-retaining rings are now on the parts table.

Step 6. Remove the outer idler bearing to reach the faulty inner bearing. Press G to set it aside for reuse.

Step 7. Remove the faulty inner idler bearing. Press G to discard it from this repair; use the new replacement bearing next.

Step 8. Pick up the part labelled New replacement · timing idler bearing from the table. Fit it into the empty inner bearing position inside the pulley.

Step 9. Refit the original outer bearing into the remaining idler bearing seat.

Step 10. Return the first labelled split ring to its groove inside the pulley opening. It keeps the bearing from sliding out.

Step 11. Return the second labelled split ring to the other groove. Both bearings are retained, and the idler unit is complete again.

Step 12. Pick up the rebuilt timing idler and fit it back onto its shaft, against the retained inner shaft circlip.

Step 13. Refit the outer shaft circlip to secure the rebuilt idler.

Step 14. Refit the timing belt around its original pulleys and idlers to complete the repair.
