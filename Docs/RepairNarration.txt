TIMING IDLER BEARING REPLACEMENT

A bearing lets the guide pulley rotate freely on a fixed shaft. Remove only the parts needed to reach the faulty bearing, replace it, then rebuild the same connections. Set removed parts aside using the drop control; on PC this is G.

Step 1. We will replace one bearing inside a timing-belt guide pulley. A worn bearing can stop the pulley turning freely. First find the toothed belt on the end opposite the flywheel. Lift it off the large pulleys and small idlers, then set it on the parts table. This gives access to the faulty idler.

Step 2. Find the first small timing idler, labelled timing idler one. The split ring on the exposed end of its shaft stops the pulley sliding off. Remove this outer ring and set it aside. Leave the inner ring, behind the pulley, on the shaft.

Step 3. Slide the whole first idler off its fixed shaft. Carry it to the small work platform and set it down with its centre opening accessible. We are removing the pulley and its internal bearings together, not removing the shaft from the engine.

Step 4. Look inside the pulley opening. The two rings in grooves inside this opening hold the bearings in the pulley. Remove the first internal retaining ring and set it aside for reuse. These internal rings are different from the ring you removed from the engine shaft.

Step 5. Remove the second internal retaining ring from the other end of the pulley opening. Set it aside with the first. Both bearings are now free of their retaining rings, so we can reach the damaged bearing without forcing it past a clip.

Step 6. Remove the outer bearing ring from the pulley centre and set it aside for reuse. Its balls let the pulley rotate around the fixed shaft. This bearing is still good; removing it gives access to the inner bearing that needs replacement.

Step 7. Remove the faulty inner bearing from the now-open pulley centre. Set this damaged bearing aside separately. We will use a new bearing of the same size instead of putting the faulty one back.

Step 8. Pick up the part labelled New replacement timing idler bearing. Fit it into the empty inner bearing position inside the pulley centre. Its outer ring seats in the pulley, and its small centre hole lines up with the pulley axis.

Step 9. Pick up the original good outer bearing. Refit it into the remaining seat at the outer end of the pulley opening. The two small bearing holes must line up so the engine's fixed shaft can pass through both.

Step 10. Refit the first internal split ring into its groove inside the pulley opening, just outside the bearing. It stops the bearing sliding out. Keep the centre opening clear; the ring sits at the edge of the opening.

Step 11. Refit the second internal split ring in the groove at the other end of the opening. Both bearings are now retained. The rebuilt idler contains one new bearing and one reused bearing, held by the original two internal rings.

Step 12. Pick up the rebuilt idler as one unit. Slide its bearing holes over the original shaft on the engine's timing-idler plate. Move it inward until it reaches the inner shaft ring that you left in place. Leave the outer shaft groove visible.

Step 13. Refit the outer shaft clip into the exposed groove just beyond the pulley. The two shaft clips now keep the whole idler on the engine, while its internal bearings allow it to turn.

Step 14. Refit the toothed timing belt around the lower crankshaft pulley, the two upper camshaft pulleys and the small guide idlers. You have replaced the faulty bearing and reused the good parts. The belt again links piston movement to valve movement. This simulation shows the fitted route; real timing and tension checks require engine-specific instructions.
